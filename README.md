# The Ithaca Journal | 伊萨卡手记

![The Ithaca Journal home screen](assets/readme/ithaca-journal-home.png)

> Development build: the left entrance opens the visual novel, the right one opens the interactive writing tool.

*The Ithaca Journal* is a Research through Design project that couples a visual novel and an interactive writing tool inside one work. Current development focuses on finishing the novel's story, staging, and playable build.

## 1. The story we are writing

A short, authored visual novel. A young developer comes to an unfamiliar city for a job interview, carrying a journal application he built earlier — *The Ithaca Journal* — as his portfolio piece. Across the story he reopens it, polishes it, presents it, and the project ends where there is no next version. The writing tool appears inside that story: the player watches it being used, uses it, and watches it being judged. The story, its staging and a playable build are what we are still making.

## 2. How the story gets written

- **Written as text first.** The story is written and revised as prose in an authoring source of its own, kept separate from the application that displays it: the application presents the story, it does not contain it.
- **Structure holds while words change.** Scenes are addressed by their place in the structure rather than by their titles, so rewriting, retitling or reordering never invalidates the progress a reader has already saved.
- **Two worlds are declared, not implied.** Every scene states which world it belongs to, and images, chapter cards and animation are chosen when the scene is presented instead of being fixed in the prose — so reality and the design draft can be kept apart without rewriting the text.
- **Checked before it is played.** The story is validated as a whole — entry, links, reachability, endings, unfinished placeholders — before it can be played through or handed to someone else.
- **Authored, not a sandbox.** Choices change local feedback and pacing, but they do not branch into several different products.

## 3. Why those two are one thing

The pages the developer writes inside the story come from the very tool the author uses to write the story. The journal produces the fiction, and the fiction puts the journal on display — a meta-fiction and a meta-tool at once. That is why "what story" and "how it is written" are not separate questions here: the authoring form is itself the narrative device.

## 4. Inheritance: what this version carries over, and where

The tool is inherited rather than invented for the story, and the inheritance is deliberately made visible in three places.

**From prototype to story.** The desk, topic board, mailbox, and bookshelf — including the earlier attempt to re-arrange them into a themed journey — are not thrown away. They are carried into the novel as *the developer's own design draft*, so that the previous version is not discarded history but something the story can look at, use, and doubt.

**Two worlds that must stay apart.** Reality and the design draft are kept distinct in text and in staging: the character lives in an ordinary rented room in the city, while the journal's own room exists only inside the design draft. The two spaces never merge. That separation is what keeps the inherited tool honest: it is a designed object inside the story, not the world the character actually lives in.

**Kept as history, not as naming.** The product release, the content revision of the Journey, and the save schema stay distinct, and none of them names the story. The early desktop prototype also survives as the [`legacy`](https://github.com/666feiyu666/Ithaca-Journal/tree/legacy) branch — historical reference, not the development line.

The home screen keeps two independent entrances:

| Entrance | What it opens |
| --- | --- |
| **Visual novel: start the journey** | The authored developer meta-story. |
| **Writing tool: The Ithaca Journal** | The desk, the topic board, the mailbox, and the bookshelf, used on their own. |

This is the design direction currently in production, not a research finding already backed by participant evidence.
